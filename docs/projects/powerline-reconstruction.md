# Powerline Reconstruction

[Documentation index](../README.md) · [LiDAR evidence article](../articles/lidar-evidence.md)

**Project:** spotlite-powerline-reconstruction. **Role:** exploratory pipeline development and delivery tooling. **Status:** private research-oriented package.

## Problem and project scope

LiDAR observations can support reconstruction of cable geometry, but sparse and ambiguous evidence makes unconstrained completion misleading. A useful workflow needs to preserve measured points and identify any geometry generated by the algorithm.

The project combines input interpretation, candidate grouping, adaptive fitting, diagnostic outputs and independent product readback. Conservative and exploratory results have distinct meanings.

## My contribution

My work includes the initial reconstruction pipeline, input auditing, evidence-aware processing, traceable output handling and package resources. I have also worked on the container, CI checks and documentation around its execution.

The engineering contribution is broader than fitting a mathematical curve: it includes deciding when the available evidence does not support a result and ensuring that exported files preserve the distinction between observation and reconstruction.

## Conceptual architecture

LAS/LAZ input → coordinate and class audit → supported candidates → fitting and rejection checks → preserved observations plus separately marked generated geometry.

**Technologies:** Python, numerical geometry, LAS/LAZ tooling, automated tests and container packaging.

## Engineering decisions and limits

Point-cloud size is not the same as conductor support. Relevant evidence needs to be assessed locally. An unresolved span can be a more defensible result than a smooth curve generated across an unsupported gap.

Original attributes and coordinates should remain available for inspection. Generated points must carry machine-readable provenance, since a display color alone may be lost when another tool opens the file.

The workflow is bounded and exploratory. It is not presented as a distributed service for arbitrarily large point clouds, a universal reconstruction method or a source of certified infrastructure geometry.

This page excludes source surveys, internal fitting settings and benchmark results. The accompanying article discusses general principles of evidence, missing terrain information and independent readback. Those explanations help a reader understand the engineering without receiving operational configurations or a copy of the private implementation.
